Troy chapters

Keep poetry and evidence in conversation

Troy matters because myth, memory and archaeology overlap—without perfectly matching.

Literary tradition

The Iliad

A poem about anger, honor and mortality set during the Trojan War. It focuses on a short episode, not the fall of the city.

Archaeology

Hisarlık

A real, long-lived settlement controlling approaches to the Dardanelles, with major Bronze Age fortifications and destruction horizons.

Popular legend

The Wooden Horse

The horse belongs to the wider Trojan cycle and later retellings. No archaeological evidence proves the device existed.

Reasonable conclusion

A memory of conflict

Stories may preserve echoes of Late Bronze Age warfare, but the surviving poems were shaped over generations.

What archaeology can support

  • A strategic settlement existed here.
  • It was fortified and repeatedly rebuilt.
  • Some phases ended violently.
  • Later Greeks and Romans strongly identified the mound with Troy.

What it cannot prove

  • That Achilles or Hector were historical individuals.
  • That one ten-year war unfolded exactly as later tradition tells it.
  • That a wooden horse was used.
  • That a single layer can be labeled “Homer’s Troy” with certainty.
