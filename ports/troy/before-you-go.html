Before You Go

A practical and interpretive primer for your four-hour excursion.

Troy chapters

Arrive with the right expectations

Ephesus impresses through streets and façades. Troy is different: low walls, trenches and changes in masonry reveal a much longer story.

Bring

Hat, water, sunscreen, secure shoes and a light layer for the coach.

Expect

Exposed paths, uneven stone and limited shade near the archaeological remains.

Listen for

Troy VI, Troy VII and Schliemann. These labels anchor most of the interpretation.

Do not rush

The defensive walls and excavation cuts matter more than the replica horse.

The 10-minute briefing

Troy is Hisarlık.

The archaeological mound overlooks the plain near the Dardanelles.

There was no single Troy.

Archaeologists distinguish multiple cities and many rebuilding phases.

Homer wrote poetry, not a field report.

The Iliad preserves cultural memory but cannot be read as a literal excavation guide.

The site is meaningful because of sequence.

Notice how fortifications, house plans and public buildings change through time.

Pacing: stay close enough to hear layer identifications. Once you lose the chronology, the stones become difficult to decode.
